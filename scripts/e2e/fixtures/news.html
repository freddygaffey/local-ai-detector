The term "supervolcano" implies a volcanic center that has had an eruption of magnitude 8 on the Volcano Explosivity Index (VEI), meaning that at one point in time it erupted more than 1,000 cubic kilometers (240 cubic miles) of material. Eruptions of that size generally create a circular collapse feature called a caldera.

The largest eruption at Yellowstone (2.1 million years ago) had a volume of 2,450 cubic kilometers. Like many other supervolcanoes, Yellowstone has also had much smaller eruptions.

Other volcanoes that have produced exceedingly large pyroclastic eruptions and formed large calderas in the past 2 million years include Long Valley in eastern California, Valles Caldera in New Mexico, Toba in Indonesia, and Taupo in New Zealand. Taupo erupted 22,600 years ago and is the most recent supervolcanic eruption on Earth (with a volume of about 1,130 cubic kilometers).

Volcanic eruptions are among Earth’s most dramatic and powerful agents of change. Ash, mudflows, and lava flows can devastate communities near volcanoes and cause havoc in areas far downwind, downstream, and downslope. Researchers have discovered a new species of deep-sea octopus living near hydrothermal vents in the Pacific Ocean. The finding, published in a leading marine biology journal, sheds new light on how life adapts to some of the most extreme environments on the planet.

The octopus, which measures roughly fifteen centimeters in length, was observed at depths of more than two thousand meters. Unlike many of its relatives, it appears to thrive in water that is significantly warmer than the surrounding ocean, thanks to the heat released by nearby vents. Scientists believe that this unique habitat may have influenced several of its physical and behavioral traits.

The research team used remotely operated vehicles equipped with high-resolution cameras to document the animals in their natural environment. Their observations suggest that the octopuses may gather in groups to brood their eggs, taking advantage of the warmer temperatures to speed up development.

Experts say the discovery highlights how much remains unknown about the deep ocean. As exploration technologies continue to improve, scientists expect to uncover many more species that challenge our understanding of life on Earth.

City officials announced on Monday a new initiative aimed at expanding green spaces and improving air quality across several neighborhoods. The program, which will be funded through a combination of municipal budget allocations and state grants, is expected to create more than twenty new parks and community gardens over the next five years.

According to the mayor's office, the initiative will prioritize areas that currently have limited access to parks and higher levels of air pollution. In addition to planting thousands of trees, the plan includes the installation of walking paths, playgrounds, and outdoor fitness equipment.

Community leaders have largely welcomed the announcement, noting that access to green spaces can have significant benefits for physical and mental health. However, some residents have raised concerns about potential increases in property values and the risk of displacement.

Officials stated that they will hold a series of public meetings in the coming months to gather feedback and ensure that the project reflects the needs of local communities. Construction on the first sites is scheduled to begin next spring.

Lightning is fascinating to watch but also extremely dangerous. In the United States, there are about 25 million lightning flashes every year. Each of those 25 million flashes is a potential killer. While lightning fatalities have decreased over the past 30 years, lightning continues to be one of the top weather killers in the United States. In addition, lightning injures many more people than it kills and leaves some victims with life-long health problems.

Understanding the dangers of lightning is important so that you can get to a safe place when thunderstorms threaten. If you hear thunder—even a distant rumble—you are already in danger of becoming a lightning victim.

All thunderstorms go through stages of growth, development, electrification and dissipation. Thunderstorms often begin to develop early in the day when the sun heats the air near the ground and pockets of warmer air start to rise in the atmosphere. When these pockets of air reach a certain level in the atmosphere, cumulus clouds start to form. Continued heating causes these clouds to grow vertically into the atmosphere. These "towering cumulus" clouds may be one of the first signs of a developing thunderstorm. The final stage of development occurs as the top of the cloud becomes anvil-shaped.
